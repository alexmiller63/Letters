# Valentine Rodriguez Gonzalez Jr. — Letter of Recommendation

Dear Tiffany,

I’d like to recommend Valentine Rodriguez Gonzalez Jr. for a position with the Cherry Hill Santa team.

Valentine has no formal work experience, but he has qualities that I think would translate very well to this kind of work. He is remarkably even-tempered. He lives with people with very different personalities and backgrounds, including people who can sometimes be irrational or difficult, and he deals with them calmly. He is the one person in the house who seems to have no conflicts with anyone.

Valentine is also a very good troubleshooter. When something doesn’t work, his first instinct is to find the instructions and learn how it is supposed to work. If that doesn’t solve the problem, he can usually figure it out. I’ve asked for his help numerous times. When a lamp arrived in pieces, Valentine figured out how to assemble it. When I couldn’t figure out the lanyard for my pulse oximeter, Valentine put it together.

Since writing the first draft of this letter, I learned that he has also set up security cameras, Roku-type equipment, and an electronic system for an employee to punch in and out. Apparently, I knew he was good at figuring things out without knowing the half of it.

That ability could be useful on set. The camera has to be operated correctly and staff need to learn the procedure, but even when everything is being done properly, equipment can hiccup. Someone who is unusually good at troubleshooting may be able to get things working again quickly, potentially saving the company money and, more importantly, preventing a line of disappointed or angry guests who are waiting for their pictures.

Valentine is also the kind of person who would learn how the entire set operated—where everything belonged, how the camera and register worked, what the daily routine was, and why things were done a certain way. Understanding the whole operation would help him recognize when something was not working properly and determine what needed to be done.

I don’t know him well enough to make a sweeping claim that he is always responsible, but I have seen how he responds when something needs to be done. Recently, he needed to contact a government office and get an application sent to him. He understood that it was important, contacted them promptly, and got the process moving.

I haven’t seen Valentine working with children, so I can’t speak from direct experience about that. But based on how steadily he deals with adults with widely varying personalities and problems, I would expect him to be calm and patient with children as well.

The strongest practical recommendation is that the owner and manager of the house frequently turn to Valentine when they need someone to run an errand or help with something that needs to be done. He has become, in effect, their go-to assistant.

Valentine would be starting his first job, so there will be things he has to learn. But he is calm, adaptable, good at figuring things out, and accustomed to helping when something needs doing. I think he deserves the opportunity to show what he can do.

Santa Alex

# Sophia Koikas — Letter of Recommendation

Dear Tiffany,

I am pleased to recommend Sophia Koikas for a position as a Santa’s helper with Cherry Hill. I have known Sophia for many years and have had many opportunities to observe her judgment, customer-service skills, good manners, and ability to work constructively with other people.

Sophia has an unusually effective approach to customer service. She is warm and approachable without ever being pushy. She makes people feel comfortable and treats them with respect. Having worked professionally as Santa, I believe Sophia would have fit into our team. She would have been welcoming families, organizing the line, talking with nervous children while they waited, communicating professionally with parents, and helping everything run smoothly without trying to become the center of attention.

None of the helpers rushed her or interrupted. I simply waved and asked, “What’s your name?” Later, when she seemed comfortable, I asked, “What do you want for Christmas?” Afterward, her parents told me she was autistic and that this was the first year she had ever interacted with a Santa. Based on my experience with Sophia, I believe she would have shown the same patience and restraint demonstrated by my team, allowing the child to set the pace instead of trying to force the interaction.

What impresses me about Sophia is that she notices what people need. She supports the people around her, exercises sound judgment, and helps an operation succeed without seeking recognition for herself. I would have been very comfortable having her as part of my Santa team, and I recommend her with confidence.

Sincerely,

Santa Alex

# Sarah Morgeneier — Letter of Recommendation

Tiffany,

When you asked me to recommend people for this year’s Santa team, Sarah Morgeneier was one of the first people who came to mind.

I didn’t want to write a typical recommendation letter. I’d rather just tell you about the Sarah I’ve come to know.

Sarah is rather quiet. In fact, she’s somewhat laconic. If you ask her how she’s doing, she’ll probably smile and say, “I’m fine,” and that’s about it. She isn’t someone who tries to be the center of attention, and she doesn’t use a lot of words. But I’ve learned that if you want to understand Sarah, don’t pay attention to how much she says. Pay attention to what she does.

One birthday, I happened to find Sarah walking home carrying an enormous potted flower arrangement. When she was younger, she had worked for a florist and had become remarkably good at creating beautiful arrangements. I asked her what it was for, and she simply smiled and said, “Happy birthday.” It wasn’t the flowers that stayed with me. It was the thought behind them. She had invested her time and talent into making someone else’s day a little brighter, and she never made a big deal about it.

There’s another memory that has always stayed with me. Around adults, Sarah is usually laconic. But with our two cats, Gremlin and Elf, she became garrulous. She’d carry on little conversations with them as though they understood every word. She is patient, warm, and nurturing with animals.

Sarah is also direct and no-nonsense when a situation needs to be handled. During one busy Santa shift, a group of obnoxious teenage boys approached laughing and said, “Santa, please let me sit on your lap, it’s my lifelong dream.” I had to de-escalate the situation myself because I couldn’t get a manager’s attention. Sarah would have stepped in, established the boundary, and ended the disruption without turning it into a larger confrontation.

As the Christmas season becomes busier, Santa inevitably becomes tired. I can easily picture Sarah asking directly, “Santa, is it time for a break?” If I agreed, she would lead me toward the break room, telling the parents and children along the way, “Santa needs to go take care of the reindeer.”

Every Christmas season we have younger helpers, often sixteen-year-olds working one of their first jobs. They sometimes need help understanding paperwork from Cherry Hill and direct deposit procedures, and I’ve often found myself thinking that Sarah would have been the kind of person they would have been comfortable going to. She has a practical, common-sense way about her that makes people feel at ease instead of embarrassed.

That’s why I think Sarah would fit so well on a Santa team. Christmas isn’t really about the loudest personalities. It’s about making people feel welcome, noticed, and cared for. Sarah is quiet and warm, but when something needs to be handled, she handles it.

Santa Alex

# Cindy Marcos — Letter of Recommendation

Dear Tiffany,

I’d like to recommend my friend Cindy Marcos for a position with the Cherry Hill Santa team. I’ve known Cindy for a long time, and I know her much better than the other people I’ve recommended to you.

Cindy has substantial customer-facing experience. She has worked as an Uber driver, an Instacart shopper, and as a hostess. Those are very different jobs, but they have something important in common: sooner or later, you have to deal with somebody who is extraordinarily angry about something. Cindy is very good at remaining professional and calm in those situations while also being clear about her boundaries.

She is also very comfortable with everyday technology, much more than I am. She is familiar with devices such as Android phones and iPhones and uses them effectively. She also knows when she has reached the point where she needs help and is comfortable asking for it. That combination should make it easy for her to learn the camera and other technology used on set.

One of Cindy’s strongest qualities is her persistence. I know this from direct comparison. I tried being an Instacart shopper myself. I lasted a week. Cindy has done it for months and months.

Instacart is intensely frustrating work. Items aren’t where they are supposed to be. They don’t give clear instructions. They don’t answer their phones. Cindy deals with all of that, gets the shopping done, delivers it to the customer, and gets good reviews. When I think about the long days and relentless lines that can come with Christmas work, that experience means something to me.

I’ve also seen Cindy around many of her nieces and nephews, where she is very much the friendly aunt. At the other end of the age spectrum, I’ve watched elderly people become comfortable enough with her to tell her remarkably personal stories. One elderly person confided in her about having been a prisoner of war in a Japanese prison camp. Cindy has a way of being warm when warmth is needed.

One Christmas Eve, about four hours before the end of the day, the power went out throughout the entire mall. We were all tired, and for about ten minutes we struggled to figure out what to do. We didn’t know when the power would come back on, and without power our camera and normal system were useless.

Then one of the parents pointed out something we had missed. The families didn’t care whether our camera worked. They didn’t even care about the photographs they had already paid for. What they cared about was Santa.

The parent was absolutely right.

We started letting families take their pictures with Santa using their own cell phones and gave them the number to call for refunds on the photographs they had already purchased. Our sixteen-year-old helpers handled the phones. Whether someone handed them an Android or an iPhone, they knew what to do.

I believe Cindy would have understood the lesson immediately. When the line finally died down, she would have told the sixteen-year-olds, “Remember this when you go on to your other jobs. This is what good customer service looks like.”

And there is one other reason I think Cindy belongs around Santa.

I’ve known Cindy well for a long time, and I’m pretty sure she actually believes in Santa Claus. I don’t mean that as a joke.

Santa Alex
